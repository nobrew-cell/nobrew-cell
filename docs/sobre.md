# About Me

Well, around here, I go by `NOBRE`, but in reality, I'm Gabriel—which is a nice name, if I do say so myself.

I like learning things. Technology just happened to be one of them.

### The Path So Far

I've always liked computers and electronics, especially taking things apart. I was curious enough to open almost anything in front of me, though I can't guarantee I could always put it back together.

My journey into technology really picked up after the pandemic began, in early 2020. With access to a computer, I started exploring the world of software. It was all very intuitive: I would try something, it would break, Windows would throw a blue screen at me, and then I had to figure out what happened. Eventually, this also led me to Linux.

Since then, I've built several personal projects, most of which initially stayed on my local machine. After my first college project, I started using Git and GitHub. You'll find some of those projects here, while others never made it out of my computer, ranging from d20 rolling systems to apps for organizing my reading list.

Reading, by the way, became a nice escape from the screen. Once limited to what was mandatory, I now choose my own books—which was probably one of the best changes I made.

My first language was Python, my third love. It was a great gateway and gave me the freedom to explore concepts quickly. Later on, it made the transition to Java easier, which is currently my main area of study.

My first operating system was Windows 10, and my first IDE was VS Code. My first Linux distro, on the other hand, was Debian—installed on an old computer that, surprisingly, still works. That's where I really got comfortable with the terminal, compiling projects, and configuring my own environment.

As for the nickname `Nobre`, it came from my very first RPG character. I liked the name so much that it just stuck.

### Current Context and Focus

I'm currently studying **Systems Analysis and Development (ADS)** at UNIFACS.

My main focus is backend development and Java, while technologies like Haskell, C, and Assembly tend to appear more in my personal studies and experiments.

### About This Space

Even though I use GitHub as a portfolio, a lot of what I do stays on my local machine. I don't intend to publish everything here—not now, and not fifty years from now.

So, this space is just a selection. Some projects are complete, others are only partially shared, and many will probably never make it here.

Either way, make yourself at home.
